## Slices, in order

1. **Delete fence (#1303).** `sdlc.Store` gains a per-project delete fence
   kept in the same tenant KV as the bindings. `DeleteProject` writes the fence,
   then checks for a binding, then tears down and deletes, then clears the fence.
   `SDLCService.Bind` checks for the fence after it writes a fresh binding. If
   the fence is there, it removes the binding and route it just wrote and
   returns `sdlc.ProjectDeletingError` (a version conflict, 409). The two
   orders "write, then read the other's mark" mean at least one side sees the
   other, so a delete and a bind can never both succeed. A rebind needs no
   rollback: a binding was there the whole time, so the delete's check sees it
   and refuses. A fence left by a crashed delete goes stale after
   `sdlc.DeleteFenceTTL`.
   Tests:
   - `pkg/sdlc`: fence round trip, ownership (a clear never removes another
     delete's fence), staleness.
   - `pkg/service`: a bind that commits after the delete's binding check is
     refused and leaves no binding (driven through a hooked binding reader,
     not by racing); the delete removes its fence on success and on refusal;
     a stale fence does not block a bind.
2. **Docs and ship.** Remove the known gap from `docs/projects/architecture.md`,
   document the fence and the error in `docs/projects/`, tick the row's items,
   and mark the row shipped in `docs/SPEC.md`.

## Out of scope

  shipped and tested).
- Cascading delete into offboarding: delete still refuses a bound project.
